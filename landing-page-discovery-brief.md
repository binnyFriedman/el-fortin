# El Fortín Landing Page — Discovery Brief

**Status:** Narrative options for selection. No option is approved yet.  
**Target:** `site/index.html`  
**Mode:** Persuade  
**Primary conversion:** A qualified visitor submits name, email, and phone.

## 1. The job of the page

El Fortín is a directly owned real-estate investment backed by a specific apartment. It is not a fund share, a holiday-home fantasy, or an apartment-shopping experience.

The page must help a time-conscious European professional discover, in a deliberate sequence:

1. **This is for me:** I want Spanish property exposure, but I do not want to become a remote landlord.
2. **This is unusual:** I own one apartment by deed while one operator runs the ten apartments as a single building.
3. **This is investable:** The building targets approximately 7%, with a contractual 4% net floor on the apartment's sale price.
4. **This is tangible:** There is a licensed building, a specific address, construction underway, and a named apartment beneath my capital.
5. **This is accountable:** The developer stays local, remains the operator, and is the counterparty responsible for a contractual shortfall.
6. **This is worth examining:** I should review a unit, the financial model, the documents, and the contract.

The page should not explain all six ideas in the hero. Discovery is the persuasion mechanism.

## 2. Audience

The primary visitor is a high-earning professional, typically around 50–60, in the Netherlands, Belgium, or Germany. They are considering an investment of approximately €230,000 in Valencia and are unlikely to live there full-time.

They value:

- A tangible asset rather than an abstract investment promise
- Direct ownership and a short line of accountability
- Professional operation without personal landlord work
- Precise numbers, clearly named assumptions, and visible limitations
- Calm confidence rather than aggressive selling

They should identify themselves by mindset, not by demographic labels:

> For investors who want property—not another job.

## 3. Positioning

### Core positioning

> A boutique, owner-operated building that combines direct property ownership with disciplined, fully managed operations.

### Proof, not adjectives

- **Direct:** The buyer receives the deed to a named apartment, not a fund share.
- **Boutique:** Ten apartments, one building, one operator, and direct access to the developer.
- **Safer by design:** Whole-building control removes an outside community of owners from the rental-strategy decision; a contractual floor defines limited downside protection.
- **Honest:** Price components, yield denominator, fees, assumptions, evidence, and unresolved details are stated plainly.
- **Aligned:** The developer remains the operator and the developer's company owes the gap if operations fall below the contractual floor.

“Aligned incentives” is not distinctive as a claim. The continuing operator role and contractual shortfall obligation are the evidence that makes it meaningful.

### Language boundary

Do not call the investment “safe.” Property development, operating performance, and the developer covenant all carry risk. Explain the specific protections and limitations instead.

Do not imply:

- A bank guarantee on buyer advances or on the 4% floor
- A shipped investor portal
- Documentary proof of tourist-use permissions that is not in the repository
- A proven 7% operating history
- Tax advice
- Settled pooling, payout, owner opt-out, or personal-use terms where they remain open

## 4. Shared narrative rules

These rules apply to all three options:

1. The hero creates one useful question; the next section answers it.
2. The asset and its operating model appear before lifestyle imagery.
3. The 7% target and 4% floor are never presented without their basis:
   - The published percentages use the apartment sale price (*valor público*) as the denominator.
   - Furniture and estimated closing costs are outside that denominator.
   - The 4% floor is a covenant of the developer's company, not a bank guarantee.
4. Uriel Nabel is introduced only after the visitor understands why an accountable operator matters.
5. Renders and construction photography are evidence of the underlying asset, not a detour into property browsing.
6. The unit picker appears late, after the model is understood, and is framed as selecting an investment—not shopping for a home.
7. Personal use is a secondary benefit, never one of the primary reasons to invest.
8. One primary action is visible at a time.
9. The lead form is the close. WhatsApp is the handoff after submission, not the hero proposition.

# Option A — The Model Revealed

## Thesis

Lead with the unusual structure: one apartment is owned individually while the whole building is operated together. Each section resolves the question created by the previous one.

This is the clearest expression of the product and the strongest default direction.

## Hero

**Eyebrow:** For investors who want property—not another job.

**Headline:** One building earns. One apartment is yours.

**Supporting line:** Deeded ownership in Valencia, fully managed as one asset, with a contractual 4% net floor.

**Scroll cue:** See how it works ↓

No contact CTA, Uriel reference, unit inventory, or gallery in the first viewport.

## Discovery sequence

### 01 / How can one building earn while one apartment is mine?

**Headline:** Own the deed. Leave the operations to one team.

Reveal the three-part model:

1. The investor acquires a named apartment by deed.
2. El Fortín operates all ten apartments as one managed building.
3. The building's rental strategy produces the investor's income.

This section should be visualised as one clear relationship, not a feature grid.

### 02 / Why operate the whole building?

**Headline:** One operator can manage what ten separate landlords cannot.

Explain coordinated pricing, rental mix, cleaning, maintenance, and guest readiness. Then introduce the structural advantage: no outside community of owners determines the building's rental strategy.

Use one exterior or whole-building image.

### 03 / What does the investment target?

**Headline:** A target above. A contractual floor below.

Reveal:

- Approximately 7% blended operating target
- 4% net contractual floor
- Both calculated on the apartment's sale price
- Management fees and operating strategy in plain language
- The developer pays the contractual gap if operations fall below the floor
- No bank guarantee on the floor

The focal moment is a simple return range anchored to euro examples, not a decorative statistic bar.

### 04 / What is beneath the investment?

**Headline:** A specific property with your name on the deed.

Show the physical proof:

- Camino Valencia 31
- Building licence and named professionals
- Construction progress
- Representative renders and plans
- Turnkey delivery scheduled for August 2027

The gallery is restrained and annotated. Every image answers “what is real?” or “what is being delivered?”

### 05 / Who remains accountable?

**Headline:** The developer stays after the sale.

Introduce Uriel Nabel by full name as developer and long-term operator. Show him on site. Establish local presence, relevant enterprise and engineering background, and the prior El Fortín de Puzol delivery.

The point is continuing accountability, not founder biography.

### 06 / Which asset can I own?

**Headline:** Select an investment.

Introduce the unit selector with:

- Unit and bedroom type
- Useful and built area
- Sale price
- Furniture
- Estimated closing costs
- Estimated all-in investment
- Annual and monthly value of the 4% floor
- Availability

Default to a representative available unit; do not force interaction to understand the price range.

### 07 / When is capital committed?

**Headline:** Capital follows defined construction stages.

Show the 25 / 25 / 25 / 25 schedule. State precisely that the milestones organise payment timing; do not imply bank protection.

### 08 / What should I verify?

**Headline:** Examine the investment before you decide.

Use a compact diligence section for ownership, return basis, fees, floor counterparty, permissions, construction payments, personal use, and unresolved contractual details.

This is where honesty becomes visible positioning rather than a brand claim.

### 09 / Close

**Headline:** Review the asset, the model, and the contract.

**Supporting line:** Nine apartments remain. Submit your details to review the available investments directly with developer and operator Uriel Nabel.

**Form action:** Request the investment details

After submission, hand off to WhatsApp.

## Strength

The visitor understands the differentiated product before encountering price selection or personalities.

## Risk

It requires disciplined pacing. If every section becomes explanatory, the page could feel educational rather than compelling.

# Option B — Protection Earned

## Thesis

Begin with the investor's central objection: “What protects me when I buy property abroad?” Build confidence by revealing one layer of protection at a time, while naming the limits of each.

This route makes “safe and honest” the strongest positioning, but must avoid implying that risk has been removed.

## Hero

**Eyebrow:** A Valencia investment you can examine from the ground up.

**Headline:** Property beneath your capital. A floor beneath the return.

**Supporting line:** A deeded apartment in a fully managed ten-unit building.

**Scroll cue:** See what stands behind it ↓

## Discovery sequence

### 01 / Protection starts with ownership

**Headline:** You do not buy a promise. You acquire a named apartment.

Explain the deed, the specific unit, and how direct ownership differs from a fund share. Introduce the entry price only after the ownership structure is clear.

### 02 / Protection continues through control

**Headline:** The rental strategy is not exposed to an outside owners' vote.

Explain whole-building operation and the absence of an outside community of owners controlling rental strategy. Do not make unsupported claims about regulatory immunity.

### 03 / Protection has a number—and a counterparty

**Headline:** The 4% floor is contractual, not theoretical.

Explain:

- 4% net of sale price
- The developer's company covers a contractual shortfall
- The covenant is not bank-guaranteed
- Approximately 7% is the operating target, not a guarantee

The limitation sits beside the benefit, not hidden in an FAQ.

### 04 / Protection requires evidence

**Headline:** The building, licence, team, and progress are visible.

Show the address, building licence, architect, constructor, construction photography, plans, and delivery target. Renders are clearly labelled as renders.

### 05 / Protection depends on the operator

**Headline:** Accountability stays local and in-house.

Introduce Uriel only here. Establish that he lives locally, remains the operator, and delivered the prior Puzol project. Clarify that alignment does not substitute for formal diligence.

### 06 / See the complete capital stack

**Headline:** Know what you pay—and what the percentage is based on.

Present the selectable unit economics, all-in estimate, yield denominator, floor income, management fees, and payment schedule in one disciplined financial section.

### 07 / What is not guaranteed?

**Headline:** A serious investment includes the uncomfortable questions.

Surface construction-payment protection, operating assumptions, pending documents, tax boundaries, personal-use limits, and covenant risk.

### 08 / Close

**Headline:** Review the protections. Test the assumptions.

**Supporting line:** Submit your details to examine an available unit and its supporting documents with the developer.

**Form action:** Request the diligence pack

## Strength

It differentiates El Fortín from glossy overseas-property marketing through unusual candour.

## Risk

Leading with protection can prime visitors to perceive more danger. The tone must remain calm and factual, never defensive.

# Option C — Boutique Accountability

## Thesis

Lead with the short, human chain between asset, operator, and investor. The visitor discovers that “boutique” means direct access and concentrated responsibility—not decorative luxury.

This route makes the offer feel most personal and distinctive, while keeping the person out of the hero.

## Hero

**Eyebrow:** For investors who prefer a short line of accountability.

**Headline:** Ten apartments. One operator. Your name on one deed.

**Supporting line:** A boutique Valencia building, managed for income from the day operations begin.

**Scroll cue:** Follow the line ↓

## Discovery sequence

### 01 / Your side of the line

**Headline:** Direct ownership, without remote-landlord work.

Show what the investor owns and what they do not need to manage. Identify the audience through the practical realities of owning abroad: time, distance, furnishing, bookings, cleaning, and maintenance.

### 02 / The operator's side

**Headline:** One team remains responsible for the entire building.

Explain the operating scope and whole-building strategy. Establish that management is not outsourced across unrelated agencies.

### 03 / Where incentives meet

**Headline:** The operator's responsibility continues after the sale.

Reveal the approximately 7% target, the contractual 4% floor, and the developer-company shortfall obligation. State the denominator and absence of a bank guarantee.

Only now is “aligned” earned.

### 04 / Meet the accountable operator

**Headline:** Developed locally. Operated locally.

Introduce Uriel Nabel, his local presence, relevant background, sole development investment, continuing operator role, and Puzol track record.

Use an on-site photograph, not a corporate portrait.

### 05 / One building as evidence

**Headline:** Ten apartments designed to work together.

Show the building, plans, construction progress, finished specification, address, and licence. Connect physical design choices to coherent operation rather than lifestyle aspiration.

### 06 / One apartment as your asset

**Headline:** Choose the deed behind your investment.

Present the unit selector and full economics. The interaction should feel like reviewing a term sheet with an attached floor plan.

### 07 / The honest ledger

**Headline:** Price, fees, assumptions, and limits—on one page.

Combine:

- Sale price, furniture, and estimated closing costs
- Yield denominator
- Management-fee schedule
- Payment stages
- Personal-use limitation
- Contractual and documentary open items

### 08 / Close

**Headline:** Speak to the person who remains responsible.

**Supporting line:** Submit your details to review the available investments directly with Uriel Nabel, developer and long-term operator.

**Form action:** Review an investment

## Strength

It makes directness, boutique scale, honesty, and alignment feel like one coherent market position.

## Risk

The story can become too founder-led if Uriel's biography outweighs the asset and contract. The investor must remain the protagonist.

# 5. Choosing between the options

## Choose Option A if:

The main communication problem is that visitors do not quickly understand what the product is or why whole-building operation matters.

## Choose Option B if:

Qualified prospects already understand the model but distrust overseas-property promises and need unusually transparent risk framing.

## Choose Option C if:

Prospects are most persuaded by direct access, boutique scale, and knowing exactly who remains accountable after the sale.

## Recommendation

Start from **Option A — The Model Revealed**.

It owns the most distinctive product truth:

> One building earns. One apartment is yours.

Borrow one element from each alternative:

- From Option B: put the limitation beside every protection claim.
- From Option C: frame Uriel as the end of a short accountability line, not as the face of the hero.

Do not blend the three opening narratives. One discovery question must control the page.

## Decision required

Select **A, B, or C**, or request one correction round. Only then should the selected narrative replace the existing page-flow brief and drive implementation.
